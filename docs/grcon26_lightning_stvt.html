The one idea

The equalizer's field-sync error is a free MER meter.

  • MER = 20·log10(5 / fs_err_rms), ~41 Hz, read off the decoder's own equalizer — no extra block, no extra CPU.
  • 8-VSB has a ~15.2 dB cliff between perfect TV and nothing. Everything interesting lives within 2 dB of it.
  • So every decision is made against that dial: antenna pick, gain calibration (grid search on MER), guide badges, a tone whose pitch tracks MER so you can aim an antenna by ear.
  • Per-antenna, per-hour history: recency-weighted median, trusted only with n ≥ 8 over ≥ 2 days — single-day bins lie.

Say (50 s): The whole project turns on one measurement. The field-sync is a known sequence, so the equalizer's residual on it is a modulation-error ratio for free, forty times a second. ATSC 1.0 has a brutal cliff around 15 dB, and once you have the dial, every other feature is just a policy on the dial — which antenna, what gain, whether the guide should call a channel watchable.
What the dial taught us

Three measured laws that generalise past television.

  • FEC buys 0 dB at the margin. Re-running a full-traceback Viterbi on soft symbols looked like the lever; measured, the equalizer was — the cliff is an equalizer cliff.
  • Widely-linear equalization wins at the cliff edge and loses in clean AWGN (below ~17 dB), so it has to be auto-selected per channel, not switched on.
  • Fold the FPLL into the filter: −42 % CPU, bit-identical output — the kind of saving that only shows up when you can prove identity.
  • Bonus: an AM-band loop antenna (0.5–1.7 MHz design) decoded VHF-hi TV at 189 MHz — 100× its design frequency — at 16.7 dB, right on the cliff. The meter said so before the picture did.

Say (55 s): Three things I'd have bet the other way. Forward error correction doesn't move the cliff — the equalizer does. Widely-linear equalization, which exploits the impropriety of VSB, helps exactly where you need it and hurts where you don't, so it's a per-channel decision. And an optimisation only counts if you can prove the output is bit-identical — that's how the FPLL fold shipped. The AM loop story is the fun one: the meter found a decode the antenna's datasheet forbids.

What it looks like
  • Scan → guide from PSIP/EIT → watch, channel-changer, subchannel select, captions (c cycles), record to MP4, restream RTMP.
  • Live MER dashboard at ~1 Hz with FPLL lock and equalizer state; antenna picker with measured-loss badges.
  • Signal finder: pitch = MER, 880 Hz = decodable — aim the antenna with your ears.
  • Honest verdicts: "this antenna is 5 dB short here, no setting fixes that" is a valid output.
  • Lesson from the UI: melting video was our own flag, not the signal — the user's eyes beat the instruments. Instrument the output people consume, not a proxy.
