The Widely-Linear ATSC Equalizer

8-VSB carries a real signal in a complex band — it is improper (noncircular). A strictly-linear equalizer filters only the received samples r. A widely-linear one filters both r and its conjugate r* — recovering margin the linear equalizer mathematically cannot. Below: measured, closed-form MMSE bounds on a complex multipath channel.

Why it works

The physics. For a real target, the optimal WL taps are conjugate-symmetric: |w1| = |w2| — the two branches carry equal energy (see the tap map, and the 0.50 gauge). That symmetry is the improper structure the linear equalizer throws away.

Deployable. Block-MMSE re-solved on the field sync — no LMS convergence fragility, BLAS/GPU-friendly, ~2× the filter math. Cheapest high-value lever in the dossier.

Status. WL is built into the live C++ decoder (branch stvt-2.0-wl, opt-in STVT_EQ=wl) and confirmed superior: never worse than v1, 26%–3× more watchable video at the margins (stress test above). The conjugate branch engages on real OTA signals (conj_frac 0.06→0.13 adapting). One barrier remains: the parallel companion path costs ~16 OsO overflows/40 s live — structural (5 optimization passes all flat) — so v1 stays the live default. The finish line: a fused front-end block (fpll+sync+fs_check+WL in one) to zero the OsO gate. A first-for-ATSC result — and a separable module the SDR community can reuse.
